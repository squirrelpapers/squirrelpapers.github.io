The data model

How the journal is described as linked data - in DCAT for catalogues, in CIDOC CRM for cultural heritage, and how the two fit together. The diagrams are checked against the published graph on every build.

1. From YAML to knowledge graph

Everything on this site comes from a few YAML files in the repository: the journal, one file per volume, the places and the vocabulary of entry types. A Python pipeline (main.py) enriches them with the Zenodo records and Wikidata places it has cached, and produces three things from the same merged data: the web pages, the citations and the knowledge graph. The graph is then validated, queried and mapped before the site is written.

2. Journal, volume, issue, entry

The journal is a catalogue of volumes, a volume a series of issues, an issue a series of entries. Every level has its own stable IRI under https://w3id.org/squirrelpapers/, and every entry its file on Zenodo, its people, and - for talks and posters - the event where it was presented and the place of that event. Types come from the journal's own SKOS vocabulary, mapped to COAR and FaBiO.

3. The DCAT layer

The main graph describes the journal as a data catalogue in DCAT 3, validated against the DCAT-AP 3.0.1 profile of the European Union: the journal is a dcat:Catalog, volumes and issues are dcat:DatasetSeries, entries are dcat:Dataset with a dcat:Distribution for the PDF. Bibliographic vocabularies add what DCAT does not say: BIBO and FaBiO for the kind of publication, PRISM for volume and issue numbers, schema.org and FOAF for people, events and places, GeoSPARQL for coordinates, PROV for the Zenodo record an entry was derived from, SPDX for checksums. The small sqp: ontology holds only the journal's own numbering.

4. The CIDOC CRM layer

A second graph describes the same resources in CIDOC CRM 7.1 with its extensions LRMoo (works and expressions) and CRMdig (digital objects). Where DCAT states facts as properties of the entry, CRM tells them as events: an entry was created (E65 / F28) by people (P14) within a time-span (P4); a conference is an activity (E7) that used the entry (P16) and took place at a place (P7) defined by coordinates (P168). Titles and identifiers are nodes of their own (E35, E42), each with its symbolic content (P190).

5. How the two layers meet

Both graphs use the same IRIs. An entry, a person, an event or a place is one node, described once as catalogue record and once as a CRM entity; loading both files into one store joins them without any mapping. The table reads one fact in both ways. The DCAT graph stays readable on its own, and the CRM graph can be published to a CRM-based knowledge graph such as the one of NFDI4Objects without the catalogue vocabulary.

6. One entry in both graphs: 7(4), λ5

Drawn by the build from the published graphs, not by hand: the entry Brückenbau zwischen LOD und RSE with its issue, person, event, place and file - first as DCAT, then as CIDOC CRM. Its complete description is in index.ttl next to the entry page.

7. The rules the graph must keep

Before the site is built, the graph is checked three ways with SHACL: against the official DCAT-AP 3.0.1 shapes, against the journal's own rules below, and against rules for the CRM view. A deliberately broken entry must be caught by every rule first, so the check cannot silently stop working. The rules are published as sqp-shapes.ttl and crm-shapes.ttl.

ShapeApplies toRules
EntryShape
shapes/sqp-shapes.ttl		 bibo:Document
  • A DOI is written without resolver, '10.5281/zenodo.123'.
  • An entry belongs to exactly one issue.
  • An entry has at least one creator, a foaf:Person.
  • An entry has exactly one title.
  • An entry has one entry number.
  • An entry has one issue date, a full date or a year.
  • An entry keeps the order of its authors in one rdf:Seq.
  • An entry without distribution cannot be reached from the catalogue.
  • Every type of an entry is a concept of the Squirrel Papers type scheme.
  • The citation label reads like '7(4), λ5'.
  • The landing page is the entry's page on the site.
EventShape
shapes/sqp-shapes.ttl		 event:Event
  • An event cannot end before it starts.
  • rdfs:label exactly 1
  • schema:endDate at most 1
  • schema:location at most 1
  • schema:startDate at most 1
IssueShape
shapes/sqp-shapes.ttl		 fabio:JournalIssue
  • An issue belongs to exactly one volume.
  • An issue has a title in English and in German.
  • An issue has one sigil out of #, λ, §, 𝒬.
  • prism:number exactly 1
JournalShape
shapes/sqp-shapes.ttl		 /
  • Entry {?value}: the IRI must agree with its issue's IRI and its entry number.
  • Entry {?value}: the citation label must agree with volume, issue and entry number.
  • Entry {?value}: when its language is known, the title carries the matching language tag.
  • bibo:issn at least 1
  • dcat:dataset at least 1
PersonShape
shapes/sqp-shapes.ttl		 foaf:Person
  • A person is linked to at most one ORCID.
  • foaf:familyName exactly 1
  • foaf:name exactly 1
PlaceShape
shapes/sqp-shapes.ttl		 schema:Place

  • rdfs:label exactly 1
VolumeShape
shapes/sqp-shapes.ttl		 fabio:JournalVolume

8. Stable IRIs

Every resource has an IRI under https://w3id.org/squirrelpapers/ that stays when the site moves. It resolves to the page for a browser and to Turtle or JSON-LD for a machine; the same files lie next to every page.

IRIResourceClasses
/ the journal dcat:Catalog · fabio:Journal · lrmoo:F18_Serial_Work
/v7 volume 7 dcat:DatasetSeries · fabio:JournalVolume
/v7/i4 volume 7, issue 4 dcat:DatasetSeries · fabio:JournalIssue
/v7/i4/e5 entry 5 of that issue dcat:Dataset · bibo:Document · crm:E73_Information_Object
/v7/i4/e5#pdf its PDF on Zenodo dcat:Distribution · crmdig:D1_Digital_Object
/person/<ORCID> a person foaf:Person · crm:E21_Person
/event/<name-year> a conference or workshop event:Event · crm:E7_Activity
/place/<key> a place schema:Place · crm:E53_Place
/type/<slug> an entry type skos:Concept
/ontology the journal's ontology owl:Ontology

About Squirrel Papers

The Squirrel Papers by the Research Squirrel Engineers Network are a free Open Access journal hosting publications on Zenodo and the Open Science Framework (OSF), collecting Linked Open Data (LOD), Research Software Engineering (RSE), Cultural Heritage and Geo-Sciences related research papers, working papers, blog posts, posters, slides, research data and research software.

The Squirrel Papers are an official journal accepted by the "Nationales ISSN-Zentrum für Deutschland" with ISSN 2750-560X.
